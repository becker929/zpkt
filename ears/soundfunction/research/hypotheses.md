# Hypotheses register

*What the project claims, stated so that data can prove it wrong. Twenty-six entries, roughly
twice the implicit list behind part one. Each has a measure, a prediction stated before the
test, the test, and a status. The results table at the end is regenerated by
`research/test_hypotheses.py` from the data on disk; the prose statuses were written once and
say where the evidence came from.*

Status words: **supported** (data agree with the prediction), **not supported** (data do not),
**reversed** (data point the other way), **untestable yet** (needs data we do not have: stems,
listeners, more tracks), **tested elsewhere** (a page or a run already settled it).

## A. Single sounds: what decides the job

**H1. Rise time and fall time separate the struck jobs (kick, hat, clap) from the sustained ones (rumble, pad, riser).**
Measure: rise 10–90 % and time to fall 20 dB. Prediction: a threshold on the two separates the groups at least nine times in ten on real one-shots.
Test: AUC on the folder-labelled pack (struck vs sustained folders). Status: **tested elsewhere** — on the synthetic library five measures reached 96/100 (part two, stage 1); on the 3,465 folder-labelled pack one-shots the struck/sustained split is trivially clean, the within-struck split is not (H3, H24).

**H2. Sub share (energy below 60 Hz) separates the kick from every other struck job.**
Prediction: median sub share of kicks is at least three times that of hats and claps in real one-shots. Status: **supported** in every library measured: pack kicks 0.15–0.45, hats and claps below 0.01 (part three table; pack digest).

**H3. Clap versus hat is decided by brightness and mid share, not by length.**
Prediction: brightness (centroid) separates pack claps from closed hats with AUC ≥ 0.9; length does not. Status: **supported** on the pack digest medians (claps 4.5 kHz vs closed hats 9.2 kHz; lengths overlap), but the seven-measure model still misnames claps (H24), so the separation exists and the model does not use it well.

**H4. Real hard techno kicks are less tonal than generic pack kicks: the pitch tracker reads a pitch at 100 ms on fewer of them.**
Prediction: readable share below 60 % on corpus kicks, above 80 % on pack kicks. Status: **untestable yet** as stated — the corpus number is 175 readable of 215 tracks by track, not by kick; per-kick readability on the pack has not been run. Kept as written for the next pass.

**H5. The pilot's synthetic parameter ranges cover the real kicks.**
Prediction: the corpus landing-pitch inter-quartile range falls inside 35–62 Hz. Status: **not supported** — corpus quartiles 51–64 Hz, one in four above 63 Hz (part three).

**H26. The shorter the kick body, the more sub it carries (a short thump is a sub note; a long body is mid-forward).**
Measure: kick body fall to 20 dB versus kick sub share across corpus tracks. Prediction: negative rank correlation, r ≤ −0.2. Status: see results table.

## B. Where sounds sit in the bar

**H6. Kick attacks concentrate on the four beats at twice chance or more.** Prediction: median share ≥ 0.5 (chance 0.25). Status: see table.

**H7. The clap on two and four is a minority pattern in modern hard techno.** Prediction: fewer than one track in four shows a clear backbeat (pair asymmetry ≥ 0.05). Status: see table.

**H22. Hats do not favour the off-eighths over the beats in this music (the "tchak" is not where the air energy is).** Prediction: median share of air attacks on off-eighths within 0.05 of 0.5. Status: see table.

**H23. Tracks with a clear backbeat are slower (the backbeat is a house/older-techno trait).** Prediction: median tempo lower by at least 3 bpm, p < 0.05. Status: see table.

**H25. The scarcity of the backbeat holds within every label, not just in aggregate.** Prediction: no label with ten or more tracks has a clear backbeat in half of them. Status: see table.

## C. The pump

**H8. Pump depth is independent of tempo.** Prediction: |r| < 0.15 or p > 0.05. Status: see table.

**H9. Tracks with a clear backbeat pump less deeply.** Prediction: median pump lower by ≥ 2 dB, p < 0.05. Status: see table.

**H13. The pump measured on the bass stem, at the kick's own onsets, is deeper than the mix's low band suggests.** Prediction: median difference > 0 on excerpts with an audible bass stem. Status: see table (part three found +2 dB).

**H14. The sidechain's return time scales with the beat: producers set the release to the tempo.** Prediction: positive correlation between return time and beat length, r ≥ 0.2. Status: see table.

**H17. Tracks whose kick is still loud at the next beat duck the bass more (the pump makes room the kick tail did not leave).** Prediction: negative correlation between kick level at next beat and pump depth. Status: see table.

## D. Structure

**H10. Kick returns land on eight-bar lines at twice chance or more.** Prediction: ≥ 25 % (chance 12.5 %). Status: see table.

**H11. Kick drops of four bars or longer are mostly multiples of four.** Prediction: ≥ 50 % (chance 25 %). Status: see table.

**H12. Faster tracks rest the kick less.** Prediction: negative correlation between tempo and the share of kick-free bars. Status: see table.

**H21. Tracks with a confident downbeat show more returns on the eight-bar line (phrasing and section changes go together).** Prediction: the share is higher in the confident group. Status: see table.

## E. Producers, labels, time

**H18. Labels explain a real share of the variance in the pump: crews have a house sound.** Prediction: between-label share of variance ≥ 10 % among labels with five or more tracks. Status: see table.

**H19. The scene got faster between 2018 and 2026.** Prediction: median tempo of 2023–26 tracks exceeds 2018–21 by ≥ 3 bpm, p < 0.05. Status: see table.

**H20. Kick bodies got brighter over the same years.** Prediction: positive correlation between release year and kick body brightness, r ≥ 0.15. Status: see table.

**H16. Where the rumble is its own layer (bass stem within 6 dB of the drums), the kick body is brighter: the kick is freed from carrying the sub.** Prediction: median body brightness higher in the separate-layer group, p < 0.05. Status: see table.

## E2. Loudness (added after the second literature sweep, which found no loudness figure on any of our pages)

**H30. The main sections of licensed hard techno masters sit around −8 LUFS integrated.** Measure: BS.1770 integrated loudness of the four separated stems summed back to the excerpt, treated as identical left and right. Prediction, written before the run: median between −10 and −6 LUFS. Status: see table.

**H31. Masters got louder between 2018 and 2026.** Prediction: positive correlation between release year and integrated loudness, r ≥ 0.15. Status: see table.

**H32. The crest of the mix fell over the same years (harder limiting).** Prediction: negative correlation between year and peak-over-RMS, r ≤ −0.15. Status: see table.

## F. The tools

**H15. Landing pitch is chosen independently of tempo.** Prediction: |r| < 0.15 or p > 0.05. Status: see table.

**H24. The role model's failures on real hits are concentrated in claps and hooks, not kicks.** Prediction: clap recall below 30 % while kick recall stays above 70 % on folder-labelled one-shots. Status: see table.

## G. The owner's material (private lab; numbers on the bench sheet, not here)

**H27. Anthony's kick body falls to −20 dB faster than his references' by a factor of two or more.** Status: **supported** on the first run: 42–88 ms against ≥ 250 ms for every reference, with two of his five tracks the exception (HW002 and time never lets go at 250).
**H28. His mixes keep kick and rumble in one layer where the references keep two.** Measure: bass stem level against drums. Status: **supported**: drums loudest in all five of his, bass loudest in three of four references.
**H29. Making the next demo's kick from the references' side of the map moves it right on axis 1 of the embedding.** Status: **untestable yet** — this is the experiment.

## H. From published advice (see research/advice.md for the chains and sources)

**H33. Distortion regenerates the low-mid band faster than the standard post-distortion EQ cut removes it, so kicks keep audible low-mid energy despite near-universal advice to cut it there.**
Measure: kick body low-mid band share (~150-400 Hz) in the first 250 ms of drums+bass. Prediction: median low-mid share across corpus kick bodies is at least 0.15, not suppressed to near-zero. Test: compute the corpus median of kick-body low-mid share and compare against the near-zero null a successfully-cut kick would show. Sources: Industrial Techno (Beat Dissected), Attack Magazine (tier 5); Anatomy of the Techno Kick, Audiotent (tier 5); How to Make a Techno Kick, Tracksensei (tier 5). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H34. Band-split ("invisible") sidechain ducking produces a measurably deeper duck in the sub band than in the low band on the bass stem, because the feature is specifically designed to duck below a cutoff and leave everything above it untouched.**
Measure: pump depth on the bass stem, computed separately in the sub (20-60 Hz) and low (60-150 Hz) bands at kick onsets. Prediction: median sub-band pump depth exceeds low-band pump depth by at least 3 dB. Test: rerun the existing bass-stem pump-depth method per band and compare medians with a paired Wilcoxon test. Sources: Nicky Romero Kickstart 2 Manual v2.0.9, Cableguys (tier 5); 4 Tips for Mixing Low End: FabFilter Pro-MB & Sidechain Compression Tutorial, Boombox.io (tier 5). Testable now: excerpts. Status: see table where testable.

**H35. Kicks with a strong post-distortion mid-band presence show a falling brightness slope over their first 250 ms, because the corrective EQ stage that follows distortion trims the top while the fundamental settles.**
Measure: kick body mid share (400 Hz-2 kHz) vs kick body brightness slope. Prediction: negative rank correlation, r <= -0.2. Test: Spearman correlation across corpus kick bodies. Sources: How to Make a Techno Kick, Tracksensei (tier 5); How To Make Techno: Perfecting Your Kick, Toolroom Academy (tier 5). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H36. Real corpus kicks skew toward transient-forward and away from the sub-heavy "thump" that mixing consensus says real mixes are missing.**
Measure: kick body sub share (20-60 Hz) in the first 250 ms. Prediction: median corpus kick-body sub share is below 0.20. Test: compute the corpus median kick-body sub share and compare to 0.20. Sources: Q. How can I rescue my kick drum sound?, Sound On Sound (tier 5); What's wrong with my mix (hard techno), KVR Audio forum (tier 6). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H37. Because published sidechain guidance recommends tracking the kick's actual audio (a true sidechain) specifically so ducking does not continue through kick-free bars, real bass stems show little or no residual periodic ducking during bars with no kick.**
Measure: bass-stem envelope periodicity at the beat rate, restricted to bars flagged kick-free. Prediction: fewer than 10% of kick-free bars show a detectable periodic amplitude dip at the beat rate on the bass stem. Test: autocorrelation/periodicity check on the bass-stem envelope within kick-free-bar windows. Sources: Nicky Romero Kickstart 2 Manual v2.0.9, Cableguys (tier 5). Testable now: excerpts. Status: see table where testable.

**H38. Mastering-stage advice to keep the sub band un-dynamic (because club subs run near maximum regardless) sits in tension with arrangement-stage advice to make the pump deep and audible, so louder/more club-ready masters should show shallower pump, not deeper.**
Measure: integrated loudness (LUFS) vs pump depth on the bass stem. Prediction: negative Spearman correlation, r <= -0.2. Test: Spearman correlation between integrated loudness and pump depth across the 218-track corpus. Sources: After the Loudness War: Techno Mastering in 2021, Florian Meindl / Riemann Kollektion (tier 4); Sidechain Compression in Techno: 5 Effective Methods, Gearnews (tier 5). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H39. "Musical" sidechain curves leave a slow, audible fade-back-up on the bass stem after each kick rather than an instant recovery, because manufacturer sub-bass ducking curves are explicitly built with slower fade-ups.**
Measure: rise time (10-90%) of the bass-stem envelope's recovery after each kick-triggered dip. Prediction: median recovery rise time exceeds 30 ms. Test: measure the recovery slope of the bass-stem envelope following each detected duck and take the median across corpus/excerpts. Sources: Nicky Romero Kickstart 2 Manual v2.0.9, Cableguys (tier 5). Testable now: excerpts. Status: see table where testable.

**H40. Industrial kicks are described as two glued regimes -- a 5-15 ms click layer and an independently chosen 200-400 ms or 400-600 ms body envelope, given as discrete alternatives rather than a continuum -- so real kick-body fall times should be more spread out (bimodal-leaning) than a single-decay-shape kick would produce.**
Measure: kick body fall-to-20 dB time, corpus-wide distribution. Prediction: interquartile range of kick-body fall time is at least 150 ms. Test: compute IQR of kick-body fall time across the corpus; check for bimodality with a two-cluster GMM or dip test as a secondary check. Sources: How to Make a Techno Kick, Tracksensei (tier 5). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H41. Kick bodies got more frequency-segregated (fewer simultaneous layers doing several jobs at once) over 2018-2026, following the trend a named practitioner reports in his own layer count.**
Measure: Shannon entropy of the kick-body band-share vector (sub/low/low-mid/mid/high/air) per track, by year. Prediction: negative Spearman correlation between release year and band-share entropy, r <= -0.15. Test: Spearman correlation of entropy vs release year across the corpus. Sources: Perc: 5 things I've learned about music production, MusicRadar (tier 4). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H42. The sidechain network extends past the bassline to the hi-hat/percussion bus, so a true, kick-locked duck should be detectable on the high band of the drum stem, not only on the bass stem.**
Measure: pump depth on the air/high band (6 kHz+) envelope of the drum stem at kick onsets. Prediction: median high-band pump depth is at least 1 dB. Test: apply the existing pump-depth method to the drum stem's high band instead of the bass stem. Sources: Dark Techno Rumble (Beat Dissected), Attack Magazine (tier 5); Crispy Techno Hi-Hats, Audiotent (tier 5). Testable now: excerpts. Status: see table where testable.

**H43. Modern hard-techno claps/snares are placed rhythmically more like a hi-hat (off-sixteenth, avoiding beats 2 and 4) than like a classic backbeat instrument, extending the existing finding that a clear backbeat is already a minority pattern.**
Measure: share of clap/snare attacks landing on off-eighth/off-sixteenth grid positions. Prediction: median share is at least 0.4, well above the 0.25 chance level and close to the hi-hat's own 0.50 result. Test: apply the same attack-position methodology used for kick and hi-hat attacks to the clap/snare class. Sources: Thumping Techno (Beat Dissected), Attack Magazine (tier 5). Testable now: excerpts. Status: see table where testable.

**H44. Tracks with a strong, clean dedicated sub layer show tighter (less variable) landing pitch than tracks relying on a single kick's pitch envelope, because a dedicated sub layer is tuned to one fixed note rather than emerging from a decaying pitch sweep.**
Measure: standard deviation of landing pitch (Hz) across tracks, split by kick sub-share tertile. Prediction: the top tertile of kick sub-share shows landing-pitch standard deviation at least 30% lower than the bottom tertile. Test: compute landing-pitch SD within each sub-share tertile and compare. Sources: Kick It Up, Sound On Sound (tier 5); How To Make Techno: Perfecting Your Kick, Toolroom Academy (tier 5). Testable now: corpus. Status: see table where testable.

**H45. The 2-3 LU spread between published techno LUFS targets and this project's own measured corpus median is explained by release format: vinyl-oriented releases in the corpus are mastered measurably quieter than digital-only releases.**
Measure: integrated loudness (LUFS), split by release-format metadata (vinyl vs digital). Prediction: median LUFS of vinyl-released corpus tracks is at least 2 LU quieter than digital-only releases. Test: Mann-Whitney comparison of integrated loudness by release format, once format metadata is available. Sources: After the Loudness War: Techno Mastering in 2021, Florian Meindl / Riemann Kollektion (tier 4); Mastering Techno: Loudness, Dynamics, and Club Readiness, Samplesound (tier 5). Testable now: no. Status: see table where testable.

**H46. In a blind listening test, listeners cannot reliably tell apart a low-band-only ("invisible") sidechain duck from a full-band duck at matched peak gain reduction, even though the two are acoustically very different in band-resolved pump depth.**
Measure: AB/X discrimination accuracy between band-split and full-band ducking at matched dB. Prediction: discrimination accuracy is at or below 60% correct (chance = 50%) when release is 150 ms or less. Test: AB/X listening test with matched-depth stimuli built from the excerpt corpus. Sources: Nicky Romero Kickstart 2 Manual v2.0.9, Cableguys (tier 5); Dynamic Range Processing and Its Influence on Perceived Timing in EDM, Brovig-Hanssen, Sandvik & Aareskjold-Drecker (2020), Music Theory Online 26(2) (tier 2). Testable now: listeners. Status: see table where testable.

**H47. Anthony's own material, which already shows kick and rumble kept in a single mixed layer unlike his references' two-layer approach, will show an abrupt rather than gradual pump-depth decline before a breakdown, because the gradual ratio-loosening technique depends on a separately addressable rumble/bass bus that a one-layer mix does not offer.**
Measure: pump depth in the 16 bars preceding a labelled breakdown, on the owner's tracks. Prediction: the drop in pump depth within the final 4 bars before the breakdown accounts for at least 80% of the total pre-breakdown decline. Test: bar-windowed pump-depth trend on the owner's stems once available, compared against the gradual-decline pattern described in the source. Sources: Perc: 5 things I've learned about music production, MusicRadar (tier 4). Testable now: owner_stems. Status: see table where testable.
